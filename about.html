About

I was a kid who drew constantly and played a lot of video games. Computer science came later and took just as well, which is how I ended up with a CS degree and a design minor from Washington University in St. Louis.

That's most of the explanation for what's on this site. I build things about what I already care about — a publication for a game I play, a fake ranking authority for the friends I watch, a layout for a story I liked. It's a narrow rule but a useful one: it means I finish things, and it means the writing and the design get as much attention as the code does.

I also spend a lot of time explaining this stuff to people who don't have my background. I taught an AI literacy course to thirty high schoolers with no coding experience, getting them from "what's a language model" to publishing a working mobile app. Before that I was a TA for web development and intro computer engineering. Teaching has a way of showing you which of your explanations were never explanations at all.

I'm currently building full-stack products that combine thoughtful interfaces with dependable engineering — everything here was built end to end rather than assembled from a tutorial. I'm looking for a software engineering role where I can contribute across product, design, and implementation.

This site is a case in point: hand-written HTML, CSS, and JavaScript. No framework, no build step.
